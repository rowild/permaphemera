# Website copy that fits its purpose

A review of PERMAPHEMERA and a reusable brief for AI writing systems  
Reviewed: 25 October 2026

## Finding

The recurring defect is the use of promotional language in places where visitors need identification, explanation, or navigation. Repeated two-part slogans, abstract cultural vocabulary, and invitation subtitles are visible symptoms. The more consequential error happens earlier: the writer has not decided what each passage needs to do.

A useful descriptive label is **generic promotional framing**. This is a label for this analysis, not a recognised diagnostic term.

The remedy is to establish the purpose of each page and section, choose an appropriate register, and give the editor permission to keep, shorten, move, or delete copy. Removing familiar AI words alone cannot make those decisions.

These patterns describe editorial weaknesses. They do not establish who or what wrote a passage.

## Scope and evidence

I inspected the live German homepage, About page, process page, and exhibition index in a browser. The initial public web reader could find indexed pages but could not open the site. The analysis below uses the live browser observations, not an assumption that search snippets reproduce every current sentence.

This is a targeted copy review. It does not verify every underlying project claim, every exhibition record, or every advertised search capability. It does not constitute an exhaustive site audit.

Pages reviewed:

- [Homepage](https://permaphemera.at/)
- [About the project](https://permaphemera.at/about)
- [How it works](https://permaphemera.at/how-it-works)
- [Exhibition index](https://permaphemera.at/exhibitions)

The site contains both weak promotional framing and useful factual writing. Its dates, exhibition metadata, tour-availability indicators, and concrete explanations should not be flattened into a uniform rewrite.

## What the examples reveal

### The hero

The user-supplied example, “Vergängliche Welten. Dauerhaft bewahrt.”, is a balanced slogan. Its contrast relates to the project’s subject, so it is not meaningless. Its weakness as the main heading is that it leaves visitors to infer the subject and the medium. It could introduce several kinds of preservation project.

The supporting line then adds cultural atmosphere and abstraction instead of promptly establishing the practical offer. The combination makes the first screen harder to identify than a heading naming exhibitions, an archive, or 360° tours would.

An expressive line could be retained as a deliberately chosen brand element. It should not have to carry the entire explanation, and its sentence pattern should not become a template for every subsequent section.

Possible replacement:

**Ausstellungen als 360°-Rundgänge**

PERMAPHEMERA dokumentiert ausgewählte Ausstellungen in Österreich. Die Rundgänge bleiben auch nach Ausstellungsende zugänglich.

This is one workable direction, not a proposed final brand decision. The explanatory text adds scope and continued access. Unlike an obligatory subtitle under a self-explanatory list heading, it has a clear job.

### The current and upcoming section

The heading supplied by the user, “Aktuelle / kommende Ausstellungen”, is useful. It identifies the categories below. Those two categories are a real distinction, not a rhetorical two-part slogan.

The subtitle supplied by the user is:

“Entdecken Sie, was jetzt zu sehen ist und welche Ausstellungen als Nächstes ins Archiv kommen.”

Its first clause largely restates the heading as an invitation. The later clause may introduce a different distinction: an exhibition’s physical opening and its digital publication are separate events. Before deleting the entire sentence, establish whether that distinction needs explaining.

If the cards and status labels already make both dates clear, omit the subtitle. If visitors need the distinction explained, state it directly and place it near the relevant status. Do not imply that every forthcoming exhibition already has a confirmed recording or publication date.

A successful revision may therefore be the original heading followed immediately by the cards. An optional smoother heading would be “Aktuelle und kommende Ausstellungen”, but the existing wording does not need changing to solve the central problem.

### The gallery and artist sections

The homepage repeats invitations to browse immediately below headings that already identify the content. In the artist introduction, specialist art terminology adds distance without helping someone use the alphabetical index.

A gallery index usually needs a clear subject label and useful location information. An artist index usually needs names, search, and a clear account of what inclusion means if that is otherwise ambiguous. Neither needs a general instruction to become interested.

Remove redundant introductions. Retain specialist terminology when the section actually discusses artistic approaches and its audience needs that distinction.

### The process explanation

The homepage combines metaphorical framing with a short account of capture, linking viewpoints, and continued access. The actions are useful. Some surrounding language implies that atmosphere or an entire experience can be retained as if it were an object.

A more precise explanation states what is recorded, how panoramas connect, and what a visitor can subsequently view. Cultural interpretation can follow when it adds a specific argument.

Possible replacement:

**So entstehen die Rundgänge**

Während der Ausstellung werden 360°-Aufnahmen an mehreren Standpunkten gemacht. Die Aufnahmen werden zu einem Rundgang verbunden und durch Angaben zur Ausstellung ergänzt.

This describes the mechanism without inventing capture resolutions, equipment specifications, recording times, or preservation guarantees. Three process steps are legitimate when they describe three real stages. The problem is padding stages or imposing the same rhetorical rhythm across unrelated sections.

### The About page

The About page repeats balanced mini-statements and paired headings at several points. Individually, some communicate a relevant position. Repeated together, they make the outline sound like a series of campaign lines and obscure the different subjects of the sections.

A project essay has more room for interpretation than an alphabetical directory. The aim is not to remove its argument or cultural vocabulary. Give sections names that distinguish their subjects: why spatial documentation matters, which exhibitions are selected, how the index relates to tours, and how the project started.

The account of the project’s origin has a useful quality to preserve: identifiable people, a specific place, and a dated beginning. Its acknowledgements also have a legitimate personal function. Editing should preserve that substance rather than replacing it with generic institutional warmth.

### The exhibition index

The index’s explanatory search text has a practical function: it tells visitors what kinds of information they can search for. A direct instruction can be helpful here. A blanket ban on imperatives or the German formal address would damage useful interface writing.

The index also shows why factual scope matters. An exhibition record and an available 360° tour are not interchangeable. The listing contains distinct availability states. Rewriting the whole directory as though every entry were already a recorded tour would introduce an error.

A suitable page title can simply be “Ausstellungen”. Keep useful search help and availability information, subject to verification that the interface behaves as described.

### The sponsorship section

This section has a legitimate persuasive purpose. It can explain why support matters and invite contact. It needs a different tone from a directory.

The current framing spends space describing the place reserved for prospective supporters and appealing broadly to cultural preservation. A more useful draft would identify the work support would fund, the relevant relationship, and the contact route, using confirmed project information.

Do not insert an invented funding model, promise visibility, or describe partners’ obligations without evidence. If the terms are not settled, a concise invitation to discuss support is sufficient.

## A precise description of the problem

The following passage can be supplied to another AI as the diagnosis.

> The website copy repeatedly uses generic promotional framing where readers need clear identification, explanation, or navigation. Headings are shaped into balanced two-part slogans, contrasts, or poetic fragments instead of naming their subject. Supporting lines then restate the heading as an invitation, often using phrases such as “Discover”, “Explore”, or “Experience”, without adding information that helps the visitor understand the content or use the page.
>
> This produces a predictable rhythm across otherwise different sections. Abstract language, interchangeable praise, and repeated invitations make the site sound like a brochure and weaken the distinction between useful information and promotion.
>
> The problem includes content selection and structure. A section may need a descriptive heading and no subtitle. Another may need a short explanation of scope, availability, or a technical process. Automatically filling every design slot creates unnecessary copy before any sentence is edited.
>
> Evaluate these patterns in context. A two-part title, metaphor, imperative, or persuasive passage can be appropriate when it serves a clear purpose. Preserve accurate facts, useful headings, established terminology, exhibition titles, quotations, and deliberately chosen character. Judge the writing by its usefulness and suitability, not by a claim about whether AI produced it.

## A reusable instruction for solving it

The following prompt works for an existing page or for planning a new one. Supply the actual source copy or verified project information along with it.

> Act as a website content designer and editor. Write for the visitor’s task and the project’s actual identity.
>
> First establish the site’s purpose, audience, relevant facts, language, and desired relationship with visitors. Use context already provided. Ask a focused question only when a missing answer would materially change the work; otherwise state a reasonable assumption briefly.
>
> For each page and section, identify its immediate function: navigation, factual reference, explanation, editorial interpretation, persuasion, or creative expression. A single website can contain several functions. Choose tone locally while maintaining a consistent overall voice.
>
> Preserve good existing wording. For every passage, decide whether to keep, revise, remove, move, or flag it for verification. Removal is a valid final decision.
>
> Use headings that make the subject or task identifiable. Prefer a descriptive label for lists, indexes, navigation, and reference sections. Use an expressive heading only where the brief supports it and the subject remains clear.
>
> Treat subtitles as optional. Keep one when it provides necessary scope, explanation, instruction, evidence, or an intentionally requested emotional effect. Delete it when it merely repeats the heading, announces the content below, or invites the reader to look at what is already visible.
>
> Check for repeated slogan pairs, staged contrasts, formulaic fragments, abstract praise, brochure invitations, and predictable heading–subtitle combinations across the whole page. Revise harmful patterns in context. Do not ban punctuation, all two-part headings, every metaphor, all passive constructions, or every occurrence of a particular word.
>
> Use consistent terms for the same objects and actions. Keep names, official exhibition titles, quotations, and required legal wording intact. Preserve uncertainty, attribution, dates, and distinctions between a listing, a planned capture, and an available tour.
>
> Do not invent specifics to make the copy sound convincing. When information is missing, omit an unnecessary claim or identify what must be verified. Do not add fake anecdotes, opinions, numbers, testimonials, colloquialisms, errors, or quirky phrasing to simulate a human author.
>
> If implementing the copy, render optional text only when present. Do not keep an empty paragraph or generate filler to satisfy a component. Preserve useful semantic headings, form labels, accessible names, and status messages.
>
> Return a short editorial direction, an audit table showing the original passage, decision, reason, and proposed wording, and then the final copy in reading order. Mark deletion decisions in the audit only. Keep editorial notes out of the production copy. Finish with any factual questions that genuinely remain.
>
> Validate meaning, findability, usefulness, terminology, claim support, tone, and repeated patterns across the page. Do not return an AI-authorship probability or optimise for a detector score.

## PERMAPHEMERA profile to append to that prompt

> PERMAPHEMERA is a curated documentation project concerned with exhibitions in Austria. Its site combines exhibition information, galleries and other venues, artists, and selected 360° tours. An index entry does not automatically mean that a tour exists or is already available.
>
> Write German website copy in a clear, attentive editorial voice. Use terminology that helps visitors understand exhibitions and their documentation. Preserve a personal voice where the project’s origin or acknowledgements call for one.
>
> Use descriptive headings and minimal supporting text in directories, lists, search areas, cards, and navigation. Explain the capture and tour mechanism in concrete terms. On About pages, allow a developed cultural argument when it contributes substance. In sponsorship or participation passages, allow factual persuasion linked to confirmed information and a specific action.
>
> Do not turn the browsing experience into a continuous invitation to discover, experience, or immerse oneself. Do not assume that cultural subject matter requires poetic headings or abstract art language in every interface section.
>
> Keep “Aktuelle / kommende Ausstellungen” if its content fits. Delete its subtitle if the cards and availability labels already supply everything visitors need.
>
> Keep an exhibition’s physical dates separate from tour publication and recording status. Describe continued access without inventing a permanent preservation guarantee. Preserve official titles even when they contain metaphors, punctuation, paired phrases, or unusual spelling.
>
> Treat the current live site as evidence of existing wording, not automatic confirmation of every underlying claim. Identify material factual uncertainty during the audit.

## How to choose an appropriate style

The general issue is a mismatch between a passage’s communicative job and its register. PERMAPHEMERA illustrates one instance. A universal instruction to make every website terse and neutral would create another mismatch.

| Situation | Main job of the copy | Appropriate direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archive index or exhibition list | Identify and distinguish records | Descriptive headings, dates, scope, status; little introductory prose |
| Explanation of a recording process | Help visitors understand a mechanism | Concrete sequence, consistent terms, relevant limits |
| Project essay | Develop an argument or account | Editorial prose with room for interpretation and personal detail |
| Sponsorship page | Explain a case for support | Specific, evidenced persuasion and a clear contact action |
| Travel campaign | Create interest in a visit | Sensory detail and an invitation can be useful; practical information must remain findable |
| Shop category and checkout | Help people choose and complete a purchase | Useful product distinctions; explicit instructions and consequences |
| Artist’s experimental microsite | Express a chosen artistic position | Unconventional language can be integral to the work; practical controls still need clarity |

Voice is the relatively stable identity of the speaker. Tone changes with the situation. Register concerns the vocabulary and level of formality appropriate to a setting. Function concerns what the passage must accomplish. These decisions are related but not interchangeable.

A sponsorship paragraph and an archive filter can share a voice while needing very different tone and structure. Mailchimp’s published style guide explicitly distinguishes stable voice from situational tone. GOV.UK’s guidance supports descriptive headings and avoiding repetition. Their individual house rules are useful references, not universal laws for a cultural archive.

## Why literary style instructions are insufficient

An author’s name, a genre, and a historical language variety are different kinds of reference. Shakespeare names an author and body of work; science fiction names a broad genre; Middle High German identifies a historical language variety. None by itself establishes the job of a website section.

A literary reference can help establish rhythm, humour, density, narrative distance, or imagery. It does not decide whether a subtitle should exist, which facts a visitor needs first, what a button does, or whether a claim is supported. It can also prompt an exaggerated imitation based on conspicuous traits.

For website work, translate the desired effect into observable guidance:

- Use short descriptive headings in indexes.
- Allow developed sentences in project essays.
- Ground cultural interpretation in a specific feature or argument.
- Use plain names for controls and statuses.
- Keep formality and address consistent.
- Use imagery only where it contributes to a chosen expressive purpose.

Pair that guidance with a small set of approved examples from the relevant kinds of page. Include examples of headings with no subtitle, useful subtitles, explanatory paragraphs, and practical controls. A collection made entirely of hero slogans would teach the wrong distribution.

A useful reference brief says what to take from a sample and where it applies. Its facts must not be copied into another project.

## Could this become a skill?

Yes. The useful scope would be contextual website content design. An anti-slop checklist would be one part of it.

A possible name is **write-contextual-web-copy**. This document proposes its behaviour; it is not an installed or benchmarked skill.

### Proposed activation

Use it when drafting, reviewing, or implementing website prose: headings, introductions, descriptions, navigation, helper text, calls to action, statuses, and errors. It should also apply during website construction when the AI is generating text from a brief, before generic copy becomes embedded in components.

Do not activate it merely because a user asks for a story or literary imitation. Those tasks can require a different workflow. For a website with intentionally literary content, apply the functional and factual checks without erasing its artistic purpose.

### Proposed contents

Keep the core instructions small. Separate reusable editorial procedure from the facts and voice of a particular project.

| Part | What it contains |
| --- | --- |
| Core workflow | Establish context; identify local function; plan needed content; draft or edit; verify facts and page-level patterns |
| Project profile | Audience, purpose, approved terminology, voice, relevant factual sources, and explicit exceptions |
| Functional guidance | Separate guidance for indexes, explanations, essays, campaign passages, and interface messages |
| Examples | Before-and-after pairs, passages to preserve, deletion examples, and legitimate expressive examples |
| Evaluation cases | Representative tasks and criteria that check behaviour rather than resemblance to one preferred sentence |

The PERMAPHEMERA profile belongs alongside the core workflow. It should not turn archive-specific preferences into rules for every future client.

### Proposed operating sequence

1. Read the brief, existing project instructions, source copy, and approved examples.
2. Establish which claims are verified and which require checking.
3. Identify the audience and task for each page or interaction.
4. Classify each section’s function and select a suitable register.
5. Plan the minimum useful content before filling layout slots.
6. Keep good wording; revise, delete, or move weak passages.
7. Compare the draft against the source for lost meaning and unsupported additions.
8. Read the heading outline and the whole page to catch repetition that isolated-string review misses.
9. If implemented, inspect realistic layouts, optional fields, and essential interface text.
10. Return the finished copy and concise material decisions.

### Evaluation criteria

The result should pass concrete questions:

- Can visitors identify the site and the content of each functional section?
- Does supporting text add something useful or intentionally requested?
- Does deletion preserve necessary meaning?
- Do names, dates, availability, uncertainty, and attribution remain accurate?
- Are equivalent actions and objects named consistently?
- Does the chosen register fit each local task?
- Have useful personal or artistic qualities survived?
- Has the writer avoided filling missing information with invented specifics?
- Does the page avoid conspicuous rhetorical repetition across sections?

These are editorial acceptance criteria, not a machine-generated percentage of humanity.

### Suggested evaluation cases

| Input or task | Expected behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Clear index heading with a redundant invitation below it | Keep the heading; remove the subtitle |
| Index with an important but unclear availability distinction | Preserve and clarify the distinction |
| Strong existing factual copy | Leave it substantially unchanged |
| A supplied exhibition title containing metaphors or paired phrases | Preserve the title |
| A sponsorship passage with confirmed funding needs | Allow specific persuasion |
| An intentionally poetic artist homepage | Preserve its chosen expression and clarify practical controls |
| Incomplete product or project facts | Omit unsupported detail or identify the missing fact |
| A component whose subtitle field is mandatory | Recommend making it optional; do not generate filler |
| German and English variants | Preserve meaning and register without forcing matching word order or slogans |

These cases describe a future test plan. No comparative benchmark was run in this review. A well-packaged skill can still perform poorly; validation must examine actual outputs.

## Skills found in this session’s environment

I searched the skill directories and plugin cache exposed to this session, including the visible personal-skill location. I found no dedicated installed website-copy or anti-slop skill that implements the complete workflow described here.

There are relevant partial resources:

- The Documents skill includes writing-quality guidance on descriptive headings, staged slogans, promotional tone, repetition, and unsupported claims. It is a useful editorial base, but its scope is document authoring and some conventions are specific to that format.
- Writing Blocks provides presentation conventions for drafted text. It does not choose an editorial direction or evaluate website copy.
- A writing-style onboarding skill describes learning from samples. Its stated activation is onboarding, so it is not a general replacement for the proposed website workflow.

This inventory covers the environment accessible here. It does not establish which skills may be installed elsewhere on the user’s Mac or in another application.

## Recommended next use

For an immediate rewrite, give another AI the source copy, the reusable instruction, and the PERMAPHEMERA profile. Ask it to return both an audit and the finished copy. Review the deletion decisions and factual questions before implementing the text.

For repeated use across projects, package the workflow as a general skill and maintain separate project profiles. Evaluate it against both restrained informational sites and intentionally expressive sites. The desired outcome is appropriate writing, including justified persuasion and character where they belong.
