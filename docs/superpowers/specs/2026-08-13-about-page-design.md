# PERMAPHEMERA About Page Design

## Purpose

The About route explains why PERMAPHEMERA preserves the spatial experience of
temporary exhibitions, whom the archive seeks to represent, and how the curated
360° archive relates to the broader Austrian venue index.

## Content contract

- German is the authoritative source supplied by the project owner and remains
  verbatim in the locale file.
- English is a faithful editorial translation, preserving paragraph order,
  meaning, qualifications, and the distinction between curated documentation
  and the broad index.
- All ten source paragraphs appear exactly once in each locale. The final
  chapter preserves the project’s Parkschlössl origin and its acknowledgements
  as a distinct, prominent closing passage rather than folding them into the
  broader statement of principles.
- Short chapter headings organize the long text without changing its argument.

## Composition

The routed-page shell, sticky header, breadcrumb, drafting canvas, and dark
footer are shared with the gallery, exhibition, and artist routes. A large
editorial statement and the existing spatial-document composition form the
hero. The body is a field essay: restrained margin headings sit beside a
readable long-form column and inset archival dividers mark genuine changes in
subject. Display headings preserve explicit bottom glyph clearance.

The closing section offers two direct continuations: the exhibition archive
and the Austrian gallery index. It remains on parchment and uses the existing
framed button components rather than creating a campaign-style banner.

## Navigation vocabulary

The About destination is named “Über das Projekt” in German and “About the
Project” in English. It is a primary navigation destination rather than a
secondary information-menu item. The page breadcrumb begins with the literal
home destination (“Startseite” / “Home”), while the hero eyebrow names the
page's subject as “Auftrag & Haltung” / “Purpose & principles” so the three
adjacent wayfinding levels do not repeat the same word.

The shared header language selector sits immediately left of the menu control.
It is a text-labelled dropdown with archival-filtered Austrian and British flag
marks that regain their original colour on hover or keyboard focus; flags are
decorative reinforcement and never replace the language names.

## Responsive behavior

Desktop uses an asymmetric image/text hero and two-column chapter rows. Tablet
stacks the hero image above its heading while retaining the chapter margin
column. Compact screens collapse each chapter to one column, retain generous
body leading, and keep every display heading's descenders and diacritics clear.
